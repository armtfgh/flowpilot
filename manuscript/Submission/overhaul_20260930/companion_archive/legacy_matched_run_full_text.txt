# Flow Chemistry Protocol: Photocatalytic Aerobic Oxidation of Fmoc-L-Methionine to Fmoc-Methionine Sulfoxide



---



## (1) Proposed Flow Setup



**Reaction:** Singlet-oxygen-mediated thioether → sulfoxide oxidation using 4,7-diphenyl-2,1,3-benzothiadiazole as a triplet photosensitizer, with air as the O₂ source.



**Configuration:** Single-stage gas–liquid segmented (Taylor) flow photoreactor.



### Streams



| Stream | Contents | Phase | Flow Rate | Equipment |

|--------|----------|-------|-----------|-----------|

| **A** (Liquid) | Fmoc-L-methionine (0.10 M, 1.0 equiv) + 4,7-diphenyl-2,1,3-benzothiadiazole (0.01 M, 10 mol%) in acetonitrile | Liquid | 0.2 mL/min | Syringe/HPLC pump |

| **B** (Gas) | Clean dry air (21 mol% O₂) | Gas | 0.4483 sccm (≈0.12 mL/min at operating pressure) | Mass flow controller |



**Mixing:** Streams A and B meet at a T-mixer (1.0 mm ID) to establish segmented gas–liquid (Taylor) flow before entering the irradiated zone.



### Reactor



- **Type:** PFA coil reactor, 1.0 mm ID

- **Total reactor volume:** 1.6094 mL (liquid holdup ~1.0 mL; gas holdup fraction = 0.3786)

- **Residence time:** 5.0 min (defined as liquid-phase reactor volume ÷ liquid flow rate)

- **Temperature:** 21.0 °C (thermostatted)

- **Light source:** 420 nm LED module, 1.5 W, wrapped around the PFA coil

- **Back-pressure regulator:** 3.0 bar (gauge), giving ~4.0 bar absolute



### Downstream



1. Gas–liquid separator to vent spent air safely

2. Collect liquid product in an amber vessel (protect from ambient light)

3. Discard the first ~3 reactor volumes (~3.0 mL) as startup waste before collecting steady-state product



### Preparation Steps



1. Dissolve Fmoc-L-methionine (0.10 M) and photocatalyst (0.01 M) in acetonitrile in an **amber bottle** to prevent premature photoactivation.

2. Prime the liquid pump and establish air flow through the MFC.

3. Establish stable segmented flow through the reactor **before** turning on the 420 nm LED.

4. Confirm stable back-pressure at 3.0 bar before beginning collection.



### Shutdown Sequence



Turn off the LED first → stop MFC → stop liquid pump → flush reactor with 5 reactor volumes of neat acetonitrile.

## (2) Rationale for Conditions



### Residence Time (5.0 min)



The batch protocol completes in 5 min. This was adopted directly as a conservative first screen. The automated design engine initially calculated a much shorter τ (~0.25 min, representing a 20× intensification factor), but flagged severe O₂ mass-transfer limitation (transfer sufficiency only 0.19×) and poor axial dispersion (Pe ≈ 2) at that short time. The 5.0 min residence time provides a safe starting point. Literature analogies for related photocatalytic oxidations in flow show 16–384× batch-to-flow acceleration, suggesting that once baseline conversion is confirmed, residence time can likely be shortened substantially.



### Temperature (21.0 °C)



Matches the batch protocol. The chemistry plan identifies this reaction as temperature-insensitive. The thermal Damköhler number (Da_th = 0.003974) confirms negligible heat accumulation — the reactor can remove heat ~250× faster than it is generated (UA = 1.931231 W/K vs. heat generation of 0.077 W). No thermal runaway risk.



### Concentration (0.1 M)



Directly from the batch protocol. Consistent with literature analogies for photocatalytic sulfoxidations in acetonitrile.



### Tubing and Reactor Geometry (PFA, 1.0 mm ID)



PFA is transparent at 420 nm, enabling irradiation through the reactor wall. The 1.0 mm ID provides short optical path length (~0.5 mm average for an annular irradiation geometry), which is a major advantage over a batch round-bottom flask where photon penetration is limited. The small diameter also promotes well-defined Taylor flow slugs with thin liquid films, enhancing gas–liquid mass transfer (calculated kLa = 0.153 s⁻¹).



### Gas Flow and Pressure (0.4483 sccm air, 3.0 bar BPR)



At 0.2 mL/min liquid flow and 0.10 M substrate, the substrate molar flow is 0.020 mmol/min. The air flow delivers 0.020 mmol/min O₂ (1.0 equiv at the inlet). The O₂ transfer sufficiency is calculated at 11.3679×, meaning the gas–liquid mass transfer capacity exceeds the O₂ consumption requirement by more than 11-fold — O₂ delivery is not limiting under these conditions. The 3.0 bar BPR increases dissolved O₂ concentration via Henry's law (~4× vs. atmospheric) and stabilizes the gas–liquid flow pattern by preventing uncontrolled bubble expansion.



### Wavelength (420 nm)



Matches the absorption maximum of 4,7-diphenyl-2,1,3-benzothiadiazole (~400–420 nm) and the batch protocol specification.



### Catalyst Loading (10 mol%)



High by typical photocatalysis standards, but directly from the batch protocol. No reason to change for an initial screen.

## (3) Key Differences from the Batch Protocol



| Parameter | Batch | Flow |

|-----------|-------|------|

| **Reactor geometry** | Round-bottom flask (long optical path, poor photon penetration) | 1.0 mm ID PFA coil (short path, uniform irradiation) |

| **Gas–liquid contact** | Headspace exposure or balloon; limited interfacial area | Segmented Taylor flow; high kLa (0.153 s⁻¹), thin liquid films around gas slugs |

| **O₂ delivery** | Diffusion-limited from headspace | Continuous co-flow; 11.4× excess transfer capacity |

| **Pressure** | Atmospheric | 3.0 bar gauge (4.0 bar abs) — increases dissolved O₂ ~4× |

| **Light exposure control** | Entire flask irradiated; difficult to control dose precisely | Only the reactor coil is irradiated; residence time defines photon dose exactly |

| **Scale** | Fixed batch size | Continuous; throughput = 1.2 mmol/h at current settings, scalable by numbering up or increasing flow rate |



The primary flow advantages are (a) dramatically improved gas–liquid mass transfer and (b) uniform, short-path-length irradiation. Both address the two identified batch limitations.

## (4) Warnings and Flags from Engineering Validation



### ⚠️ This design is NOT engine-validated — screening is required



The automated design engine could not converge on a validated final design. The safety report states:



> **"ENGINE_FALLBACK: screen required — no usable candidates after filtering"**



**Confidence level: LOW.**



This means the 5.0 min / 0.2 mL/min conditions represent a conservative starting point, not an optimized design. The engine generated five screen candidates (S1–S5) exploring residence times of 0.25–0.5 min, tubing IDs of 0.5–1.6 mm, and temperatures of 21–41 °C, but none passed all engineering gates simultaneously.



### What to do in practice



1. **Run the baseline first** (5.0 min, 21 °C, 0.1 M, 0.4483 sccm air, 3.0 bar BPR) and measure conversion and sulfoxide selectivity (vs. sulfone over-oxidation).

2. If conversion is high (>90%), systematically reduce residence time by increasing liquid flow rate (e.g., 0.4, 0.8, 1.0 mL/min → τ = 2.5, 1.25, 1.0 min) to find the minimum τ for acceptable yield.

3. If conversion is low, increase air flow (MFC allows up to 10 sccm) or modestly increase temperature (31–41 °C per screen candidates S2/S3).



### Specific risks to monitor



- **Over-oxidation to sulfone:** The main selectivity concern. The product sulfoxide can react with additional ¹O₂. If sulfone is detected, reduce residence time or lower O₂ equivalents.

- **Photocatalyst precipitation:** At 0.01 M in acetonitrile, the benzothiadiazole catalyst should be fully soluble, but verify before running. Any precipitation will clog the 1.0 mm ID tubing.

- **Pressure drop:** Calculated two-phase pressure drop is negligible (0.009 bar), well within the 3.0 bar BPR capacity. Not a concern.

- **Feed solution stability:** The premixed substrate + catalyst solution must be kept in the dark (amber bottle/foil wrap). Exposure to ambient light could initiate premature reaction and consume dissolved O₂, leading to inconsistent results.

- **BPR pressure basis:** The design assumes 3.0 bar gauge (4.0 bar absolute). Confirm whether your BPR is rated in gauge or absolute pressure, as this affects gas compression calculations and dissolved O₂ concentration.
