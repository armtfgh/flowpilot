Why the recent grades were unreliable
For the same saved practical outputs, the recent GPT-4o judge assigned means of 0.987179 to one-shot and 0.935897 to FlowPilot. The restored GPT-5.4 evaluator assigned 0.643162 and 0.785791 to those unchanged packets. This reversal is attributable to the evaluation configuration rather than a change in the designs or their generation prompt length. Both judge model and historical judge instructions changed, so it is not a model-only causal estimate.
The manual audit found missed one-shot gas-unit and material-balance errors, unsupported deductions against FlowPilot gas calculations and confidence labels, and a real FlowPilot duplicate-charge defect that judges also missed. In one accepted Qwen assessment, the rationale said Score 2 while the stored integer was 4. The earlier blocked FlowPilot designs also contained genuine software input-boundary/identity problems. Those repairs and the later feed-alias repair were frozen before fresh generation.

What was preserved and what was corrected
The same three chemistry cases, original fourteen criteria, 0–4 anchors, applicability rules and equal criterion/judge weights were retained. The historical judge prompt and validation were restored. No applicable criterion score, critical flag or rationale was manually changed. The fresh experiment uses the same practical protocol, inventory and factual constraints in both arms, with a shared historical structured-output contract. The normal one-shot wrapper repeats its schema; this is documented and is not a pure token-length experiment. One-shot uses one call; FlowPilot keeps two initial candidates and its native review. Computation is not matched.

Fifteen saved packets were copied byte-for-byte: six historical Sonnet repeat-01 outcomes and all nine fresh outcomes from the preceding practical campaign. They were rescored as a diagnostic under the restored historical evaluator package. Their original scores and output archives are unchanged. The copied historical packets retain their known phase-insensitive worksheet limitation and must not be pooled with the new primary comparison.

Six fresh designs use an adapter that preserves raw one-shot values and the final delivered FlowPilot artifact. It does not substitute Pydantic-normalized one-shot numbers. Phase-aware time checks use the declared basis; gas reference calculations are explicitly conditional, and total carrier-gas equivalents are not automatically treated as active-reagent equivalents. Internal council traces do not earn credit. The original rubric and acceptance rules remain unchanged; a stricter current validator runs as a separate audit only.

Why the first fresh run cannot answer the architecture question
The common final-JSON output instruction was appended to the design objective, which propagated it into intermediate specialist prompts. All three translation calls returned a nested proposal envelope. The parser expected a flat proposal and silently accepted defaults from the outer envelope, losing supplied fields before review. All three preceding practical-repair translation responses had the expected flat structure. This is a harness-induced confound exposing a pre-existing parser robustness defect. It is not evidence that the same chemistry information became insufficient or that the one-shot architecture is intrinsically better. A separate solvent-loss inventory-mapping defect also blocked valid pump assignment; both original blocked deliveries remain preserved. An independently reviewed follow-up reuses all three fixed one-shot outputs and reruns all three FlowPilot tasks after routing only final serialization requirements out of internal specialist objectives, retaining scientific requirements and frozen core code. It changes neither rubric nor judging instructions and must be reported separately from this diagnostic.

Checking the hydrogenolysis findings against the delivered evidence
The delivered 1.569036 min inlet/STP index, 5.178932 min pressure-corrected channel index and 6 min liquid-only index each close with their explicitly stated denominators. Treating their difference as a geometric contradiction is unsupported. However, FlowPilot retained erroneous earlier gas/time rationale, explicitly marked as requiring review after parameter changes. That remains a real presentation defect. Its illustrative 10 mL stock basis also needs scaling to cover the minimum 12 mL commissioning-plus-collection schedule. Conversely, all three judges missed the one-shot instruction to pressure-test the assembled system at 25 bar despite a supplied BPR limit of 21 bar and its own instruction never to exceed that value. This audit qualifies the reliability of the judgments; it does not replace their integers, erase critical flags or recalculate an adjusted winning score.

Interpretation limits
This is a fixed development comparison with one generation per case and arm, not a replacement for the manuscript three-repeat benchmark or an independent holdout. The cases have been inspected during software development. Scores are model judgments and critical flags are judge findings, not independently confirmed failures. The result must be read with the per-judge breakdown and the independent content audit. It does not establish that shorter prompts improve quality, that any architecture always wins, or that laboratory performance follows directly from assessed design quality.
