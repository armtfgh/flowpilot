When preparing the same proposed first experiment, check the following points and report the relevant information in ordinary prose or tables.

Preserve the stated substrate, product, catalyst, solvent and reactive components. Distinguish chemical facts in the batch procedure from possible uses of equipment in the inventory. Explain the catalyst phase and how it is contacted and retained. Specify feed preparation, stock concentrations, stream flow rates and the resulting component molar flows and equivalents.

Connect feeds, pumps or gas controllers, mixers, reactors, pressure control, separation and collection in their operating order. Use only the available equipment and respect its stated operating limits. Identify the selected equipment clearly.

Define the volume and flow basis for each reported residence or contact time. For packed beds or gas–liquid flow, distinguish geometric empty-bed or nominal volumetric indices from liquid holdup and measured contact time. For gases, state the reference temperature and pressure, distinguish total gas from reactive gas equivalents, and show the conversion between controller flow and reactor-condition flow using absolute pressure. Check numerical consistency across the conditions, diagram and procedure.

Include applicable leak testing, priming, purge, startup, collection, shutdown and work-up instructions, with concrete controls for the identified hazards. Separate measured batch facts, calculations, design assumptions and unresolved questions. Treat the chosen conditions as an experimental starting point and explain the evidence needed to refine them; do not assume that batch yield or reaction time transfers unchanged.
