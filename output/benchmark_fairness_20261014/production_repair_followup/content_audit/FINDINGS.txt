Independent audit of the three fresh FlowPilot production-repair outputs
======================================================================

Scope
Read-only review of the three result.json files under campaign/generation and
their embedded inventory, final process graph, procedure and rationale. No
one-shot outputs or judge responses were opened. No generated design, source,
prompt, grade or campaign configuration was changed. This audit does not assign
replacement scores. Source hashes and independent arithmetic are retained in
arithmetic_audit.json and the case-specific *_arithmetic.json records.

Confirmed repairs and quantitative closure
- All three final designs report executable status. Independently recomputed
  feed totals, stage volume/time relationships and minimum stock requirements
  agree with the delivered figures. Every assigned topology equipment ID occurs
  in the supplied inventory.
- Hydrogenolysis requires at least 12 mL of Stream A for 18 min commissioning
  plus 6 min collection at 0.5 mL/min. Preparation contains one substrate charge
  of 1.512 mmol at 0.126 M. Extra startup/displacement and extended collection
  volumes are explicitly unresolved, and available stock is not invented.
- Hydrogen at 1.412002 sccm supplies approximately 0.063 mmol/min, agreeing with
  the 0.063 mmol/min substrate feed and 1 equivalent. The independently computed
  in-channel gas flow is 0.07926955 mL/min at 60 C and 22.01325 bar absolute.
  The 1.569036 min inlet/STP index, 5.178932 min in-channel index and 6 min liquid
  volume index are different, consistently labelled quantities.
- Photochemistry uses 0.5 mL/min liquid and 5.336362 sccm air. Applying 21 mol%
  oxygen gives approximately 0.050 mmol/min O2, matching one equivalent to the
  0.050 mmol/min substrate feed. Its minimum stock is 4 mL for 6 min initial
  commissioning plus 2 min collection. The 420 nm source is inventory-assigned;
  irradiation is enabled before commissioning and disabled before flushing.
- CuAAC uses a 0.3 mL packed bed at 0.5 mL/min, giving 0.6 min nominal empty-bed
  space time. The 1.2 mL minimum stock covers 1.8 min commissioning and 0.6 min
  collection. Feed preparation preserves benzyl azide/phenylacetylene ratios;
  Cu/C is represented as stationary catalyst rather than a liquid feed charge.
- In both gas cases the mixer feeds the reactor, then the BPR precedes the
  vented separator and collector. Hydrogen purge/check-valve/vent controls and
  explicit shutdown sequencing survive publication. No old separator-before-
  BPR error was found.
- Final quantitative explanations are explicitly calculated from delivered
  settings. Original quantitative rationale is retained in non-executable
  history; model scientific arguments are labelled unverified. The original
  top-level explanation has status pre_realization_audit_only. Arithmetic
  consistency does not certify chemical performance or safety.

Remaining findings, distinguished from canonical arithmetic errors

1. Hydrogenolysis still contains stale and unsupported scientific prose.
   $.final_design.decision_rationale.chemistry_notes.text contains the old gas
   value 0.0167 mL/min and 5.84 min calculation, alongside a 10-50-fold kLa claim
   and an asserted batch rate-limiting step without confirming measurements.
   This entry is visibly unverified and requires_review_after_parameter_change;
   the correct final gas/time calculations are separately authoritative.
   Therefore this is a residual interpretation burden, not evidence that the
   delivered 1.412002 sccm setting or its numerical closure is wrong.
   The stationary-components list also contains two descriptions of the same
   Pd(OH)2/Al2O3 catalyst. Neither is an extra liquid charge or quantified second
   loading, so duplicate metadata alone does not prove double dosing.

2. Photochemical downstream separator capacity needs a stated flow basis.
   Reactor/mixer total actual flow at 3 bar gauge is 1.950886 mL/min, within the
   mixer's listed 2 mL/min maximum. At the vented separator after the BPR, ideal
   gas expansion to 1.01325 bar absolute and 21 C gives
       Q_total = 0.5 + 5.336362 * (294.15/273.15) = 6.246626 mL/min.
   $.inventory_snapshot.separators[0].max_flow_mL_min is 5.0, but its reference
   state and whether it refers to liquid-only or total throughput are absent.
   If it is an actual total-throughput rating, the proposed outlet exceeds it.
   This conditional capacity concern requires clarification; it is not an
   established inventory violation under an unspecified rating convention.
   The unverified council justification also calls 0.5 mL/min the maximum pump
   rate, whereas the supplied pump maximum is 1.0 mL/min. The setpoint itself is
   within range. Older 5.0 sccm prose remains in unverified chemistry_notes.

3. CuAAC needs an explicit plan for hot product depressurization/collection.
   The final graph connects the 150 C packed bed directly to the 20 bar BPR and
   vented collector. No downstream cooling unit or operating cooling step is
   specified before normal product collection; the procedure only calls for
   cooling during shutdown. Consequently effluent temperature, potential flash
   behavior and vapor management at normal collection remain unresolved. This
   observation does not presume that the actual outlet necessarily stays at
   150 C, nor does it invent a missing cooler in the inventory.
   Its unverified council_selection recommends three one-minute fractions after
   at least 3 min equilibration, whereas the authoritative minimum procedure is
   1.8 min plus 0.6 min. The stock instructions explicitly require scaling for
   longer collection, so the minimum is internally consistent; following that
   optional council sampling recommendation would need at least 3 mL feed,
   before measured allowances, rather than the minimum 1.2 mL recipe basis.

4. Across the cases, numerical design completeness remains distinct from a
   fully commissioned SOP. Final temperatures and equipment are specified, but
   explicit instructions to establish the reactor temperature under solvent
   flow before reactive feed introduction remain generic. Packing details and
   catalyst-bed loading/voidage are not established by an assigned reactor name.
   The outputs retain these unknowns in their scientific/validation discussion;
   arithmetic closure should not be described as resolving them.

Interpretation
The repairs demonstrably corrected stock/schedule mismatch and control-step
publication and made explanation authority explicit. The new outputs still
contain chemical/operating questions that require research judgement. These
findings should be reported without changing this frozen campaign or its scores.
